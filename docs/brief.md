Project Brief: Self-Learner Goal Tracker
Problem
Self-learners who really want a market skill keep postponing today's work across the week, so they never complete a full goal. They get bored after a short time even when they have the energy, lose hours to their phones, and don't know their next step to keep developing the skill.
Who it's for
The first version targets self-learners building a skill the market values, such as an AWS certification or React. Some have a fixed exam date; others set their own target and deadline. Students in school or university with daily homework are a later audience.
Why current tools fail
The tools carry no consequence, so learners ignore them.
• Apps (to-do lists, focus timers, phone blockers): learners ignore the reminders or switch the blockers off.
• Studying with friends: friends are too busy to meet daily or weekly, and nobody keeps anyone accountable.
The consequence that would work: seeing the risk of failing the exam or missing the deadline, and someone the learner chose finding out when they slack.
What success means
A learner succeeds when they reach the goal they set at the start, by its deadline, and always know their next step.
The app: how it works
A web app for mobile and desktop browsers that turns a goal into daily tasks and warns the learner, and a person they chose, when they slip.
1. The learner sets a goal: an exam date (AWS) or their own target and deadline (finish a React course in four weeks).
2. They break it into small tasks, each one finishable in a day (a lesson or chapter).
3. They mark tasks done (self-reported in version 1).
4. The app compares planned progress with actual progress against the deadline.
5. It shows where the delay is and the risk of missing the goal.
6. It shows the next step: the next unticked task, the learner's own choice, or an AI suggestion based on their career and plans.
7. Any day with no task completed, it emails the learner and their chosen person. That person confirms by email before receiving anything.
The risk figure is an estimate from progress against plan, not a true prediction of passing.
